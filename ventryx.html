Opening bell · breakout scanner

A classic day-trading method: let the first 15 minutes settle, then trade the stocks that break out of that early range. Every setup is tracked live, so you can see which reached their target and which were stopped out.

1 · Mark the range

From 14:30 to 14:45 UK time, note each stock’s highest and lowest price. That box is the “opening range”.

2 · Wait for a breakout

If the price pushes above the box, that’s a buy signal (long). If it drops below, that’s a short. Stocks that gapped and opened on heavy volume rank highest.

3 · Exit automatically

Each trade has a target twice as far away as its stop. Whichever is hit first ends it; anything still open is closed at 21:00 UK time.

Auto-trader · opening bell on autopilot

Connects the breakout scanner to a broker account at Alpaca. Between 09:45 and 11:00 New York time (14:45–16:00 UK) it buys fresh breakouts as bracket orders, so the exit target and the stop sit on the broker’s servers and each trade closes itself. If Claude is connected it reads each stock’s latest headlines first and vetoes trades with obvious news risk. Everything still open is closed by 15:40 New York time; nothing is held overnight.

How it works

Scan

  • About 100 large, liquid US-listed stocks.
  • Every 15 minutes while the US market is open (14:30–21:00 UK time), plus once after the close.
  • This page checks for a new scan every minute and highlights changed rows.

Opening bell

  • Opening range = high and low of the first 15 minutes.
  • Ranked at 09:45 New York time on the gap, opening volume against normal and the 20-day trend, never on how the trade turned out.
  • If a bar touches both the stop and the target, it counts as stopped.

Long term

  • Needs 8+ analysts and a profit over the last 12 months.
  • Score weights analyst upside, rating, 6-month momentum, revenue growth, margin, the 200-day trend and volatility.
  • Articles come from Yahoo Finance’s news feed for each stock.

Where to trade

Long ideas can be bought as shares through any FCA-authorised broker offering US stocks, such as Interactive Brokers, Trading 212, IG, Hargreaves Lansdown, Freetrade or eToro. Short ideas need a product that profits from falls, such as CFDs or spread bets. Ventryx has no link to any broker; check any firm on the FCA Register.

Important: Ventryx is a work-in-progress research tool, not financial advice and not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. Setups are generated automatically from delayed market data and published analyst forecasts, and the scanner’s results are tracked on paper. The auto-trader only trades the owner’s own broker account, in Alpaca’s simulated paper mode unless live trading is switched on deliberately. Past results don’t predict future ones. Prices can fall as well as rise and you could lose money. Day trading is high risk. CFDs and spread bets are complex, leveraged products and a high proportion of retail accounts lose money using them.
