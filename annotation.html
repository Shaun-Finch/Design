🔍What I evaluate

✍️Written output

Evaluating model-generated prose against strict scoring rubrics — identifying generic, padded and formulaic phrasing, and specifying the revisions that lift output to a natural, human standard.

  • Rubric application and consistency across large batches
  • Tone, register and voice assessment
  • Written justification for every score

💻Front-end code

Auditing AI-generated HTML for structural and semantic issues — assessing heading hierarchy, nesting and overall code health, and reporting markup that is visually convincing but semantically empty.

  • Document structure and semantic correctness
  • Heading hierarchy and nesting integrity
  • General code health of generated markup

📐Documents and layout

Reviewing AI-produced web pages and PDF documents against established design principles — grading typography, spacing, visual hierarchy and layout, and writing the feedback used to train models on formatting and document structure.

  • Typography, spacing and grid
  • Visual hierarchy and reading order
  • Formatting and document structure

⚙️How the work runs

Every engagement comes with its own criteria and its own turnaround. The job is to apply those criteria identically on the first item and the last, at speed, while writing feedback specific enough to be actionable. Work is quality-scored, so consistency is the whole game.

I work to detailed rubric criteria under tight turnaround times, across multiple concurrent specialist projects, and have onboarded onto new evaluation task types as they have been introduced.

  • LLM output evaluation & RLHF
  • Rubric-based scoring and annotation
  • Natural-language quality review
  • HTML structure & code-health auditing
  • Document and web layout evaluation
  • High-level domain evaluation (Visual & UI)
  • Content evaluation under strict time limits

👁️Why a designer

Most evaluation work is bottlenecked on people who can tell the difference between output that is nearly right and output that is right. For anything visual — a page, a document, a generated layout — that difference is not something you can reason your way to from a checklist. You either recognise it or you do not.

Eleven years building moving image and interfaces for the BBC, Universal Studios, Paramount Pictures and LIV Golf, delivering to fixed broadcast and distribution deadlines where typography, hierarchy and timing had to be right first time. That is where the recognition comes from. Strongest where evaluation needs trained visual expertise rather than a checklist.
