What I evaluate

Three areas, each with its own rubric and its own failure modes.

Written output, against strict rubrics

Scoring model-generated prose on detailed criteria and writing the reasoning behind every score. Much of it is identifying the familiar failure modes — padding, hedging, formulaic structure, sentences that are fluent but say nothing — and specifying the revision that lifts the text to a natural, human standard.

  • Rubric application and consistency across large batches
  • Tone, register and voice assessment
  • Written justification for every score

Front-end code, for structure and health

Reading generated HTML and reporting what is structurally wrong with it: heading hierarchy that does not describe the document, nesting that will not survive a change, markup that is visually convincing and semantically empty.

  • Document structure and semantic correctness
  • Heading hierarchy and nesting integrity
  • General code health of generated markup

Documents and layout, on design principles

Assessing web pages and PDFs that models produce, and writing the feedback used to train them on how a document should be set. This is where the design background does the work — grading typography, spacing, alignment and visual hierarchy against principles rather than taste, and explaining the fix in terms a model can learn from.

  • Typography, spacing and grid
  • Visual hierarchy and reading order
  • Formatting and document structure

How the work runs

Every engagement comes with its own criteria and its own turnaround. The job is to apply those criteria identically on the first item and the last, at speed, while writing feedback specific enough to be actionable. Work is quality-scored, so consistency is the whole game.

I hold multiple concurrent specialist projects and have onboarded onto new evaluation task types as they have been introduced. I work to a ten-hour weekly minimum with capacity for considerably more.

  • Rubric-based scoring

  • HTML structure auditing
  • Document and layout evaluation
  • Visual and UI domain expertise

Why a designer

Most evaluation work is bottlenecked on people who can tell the difference between output that is nearly right and output that is right. For anything visual — a page, a document, a generated layout — that difference is not something you can reason your way to from a checklist. You either recognise it or you do not.

I spent eleven years building moving image and interfaces for clients who noticed a two-pixel misalignment and a frame of bad timing: the BBC, Universal Studios, Paramount, LIV Golf. That is where the recognition comes from, and it is the thing I am actually selling here.
