halvia://ward/early-warning

Spot the patient who is quietly getting worse

NEWS2 is the early warning score used across UK hospitals. Scoring it is simple; noticing the trend across a busy shift, and escalating clearly, is the hard part. This concept scores observations live, watches the trend and drafts the escalation message for the nurse to check and send.

The problem

Deterioration is often visible in the observations hours before a crisis, but it's spread across charts and shift changes. Escalation calls can be rushed or unclear.

What I'd build

A bedside view that scores as obs are entered, highlights rising trends across shifts, and drafts an SBAR-style escalation the nurse edits and sends from the same screen.

What we'd measure

Time from a trigger score to review, missed escalations, nurse-rated clarity of calls, and alert fatigue (alerts per shift that staff felt were useful).

halvia://ward/handover

Turn rushed notes into a safe handover

Shift handover is where details get lost. Paste free-text notes and the assistant sorts them into SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation), pulls out the to-do list, and raises safety flags, including a medicine and allergy clash hidden in the first example.

Who it helpsNurses and junior doctors at shift changeFinal callHanding-over clinician approves each section

Safety flags

    SBAR handover click any section to edit, then approve it

    The problem

    Handovers vary by person and by how tired they are. Important items (an allergy query, a near-miss fall, a pending blood test) can sit in a paragraph and get missed.

    What I'd build

    A handover tool that structures notes into SBAR, cross-checks medicines against allergies, and makes the outgoing nurse approve each section, with a record of what was handed over.

    What we'd measure

    Handover time, items missed in audit, incidents linked to handover, and whether incoming staff feel they have what they need.

    halvia://records/clinical-coding

    Help coders get from discharge summary to codes

    Clinical coding turns each hospital stay into standard codes that drive funding, research and planning. In England that means ICD-10 for diagnoses and OPCS-4 for procedures. This assistant suggests ICD-10 codes, shows the exact sentence behind each one, and spots conditions that were ruled out, so they aren't coded by mistake.

    Who it helpsClinical coders and coding auditorsFinal callAccredited clinical coderMy backgroundProduct explainer for the CHKS clinical coding system

    Discharge summary synthetic

    Evidence view tap a highlight

    Highlighted: a suggested code. Struck through: mentioned but negated ("no evidence of", "ruled out"), so not coded.

    Suggested ICD-10 codes

    Code titles follow the WHO ICD-10. This demo knows about twenty common diagnoses; a real tool would use the full classification, the national coding standards and your trust's local rules.

    The problem

    Coders read long, unstructured notes under time pressure. Missed or wrong codes affect funding and the data the NHS uses to plan, and backlogs build quickly.

    What I'd build

    A suggestion panel inside the coder's existing system: evidence for every code, negation and uncertainty spotted, and one-click accept or reject that feeds an audit trail.

    What we'd measure

    Episodes coded per hour, audit accuracy, depth of coding (co-morbidities captured), and how often coders override suggestions, and why.

    halvia://outpatients/missed-appointments

    Fewer empty clinic slots, without penalising patients

    Missed appointments ("DNAs", for "did not attend") waste clinic time and delay care. This concept estimates who might struggle to attend, explains why in plain terms, and suggests help: a reminder, a video slot, or a nearer date. It's never used to overbook or move anyone down a list.

        The problem

        Every empty slot is time a patient on the waiting list could have used. Blanket reminders help, but the people most likely to miss often need a different kind of help.

        What I'd build

        A daily list for booking teams showing who may need support and why, with reminder, video and rebooking actions built in, and freed slots offered back to the waiting list.

        What we'd measure

        DNA rate by clinic and by patient group (to check it's fair), slots refilled, patient-reported ease of rebooking, and staff time per list.

        halvia://patients/plain-english

        Clinic letters patients can actually read

        Clinic letters are usually written for other clinicians. This concept rewrites one for the patient: jargon explained, medicines in a table, and next steps up front. It measures the reading age before and after, and can read the letter aloud.

        Who it helpsPatients, carers and the clinicians writing lettersFinal callThe clinician signs off the patient version

        Clinician's letter edit me

        Patient version

        The problem

        Many adults struggle with health information written at a high reading level. Patients leave clinic unsure what was found or what to do next, and call the hospital to ask.

        What I'd build

        A letter assistant inside the clinician's workflow that drafts a patient version alongside the GP letter, with a glossary, medicines table, reading-age check and translation and audio options.

        What we'd measure

        Reading age of letters sent, patient understanding (short follow-up survey), follow-up calls to the clinic, and the extra clinician time per letter.

        Principles

        How I build AI for care

        Six rules every Halvia project follows, written for clinical safety officers and IG teams as much as for designers.

        01A person makes the call

        AI drafts, flags and suggests. A named member of staff approves anything that affects care, and the interface makes that step obvious.

        02Show the why

        Every flag links to the sentence, number or rule behind it. If staff can't see why, they can't trust it or challenge it.

        03Privacy first

        Least data, for the shortest time, in the right place. Designed with UK GDPR, data protection impact assessments and the NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit in mind.

        04Safety is designed in

        Hazards logged from the first sketch, so your clinical safety officer can work to DCB0129 and DCB0160, and the DTAC assessment has what it needs.

        05Accessible to everyone

        Plain English, reading-age checks, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard support, and layouts that work on a ward PC and a phone.

        06Measure, then trust

        Model outputs are scored against clinician-written rubrics before and after launch, and checked for fairness across patient groups.
